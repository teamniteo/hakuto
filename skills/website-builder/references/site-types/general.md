# General Site Type

Content strategy reference for company/organization sites (agencies, studios, firms, consultancies, service businesses). Section composition and ordering are per-project decisions driven by the chosen direction and dials (see `design-craft.md`); this file defines the *jobs*, not the layout.

## Core Characteristics
- **Information-focused**: build credibility, explain the organization
- **Company-centric**: people, expertise, and track record are the hero
- **Relationship-driven**: the goal is inquiry and conversation, not instant conversion
- **Trust-building**: authority, experience, reliability

## When to Use
- User mentions: company, organization, agency, studio, firm, consultancy
- Content indicates: services offered, team, company history, client work
- NOT for software products with signup flows — use SaaS instead

---

## Content Jobs (requirements, not order)

**Homepage must:**
- State what the company does and who it serves, immediately — with a consultative CTA ("Get in touch", "Schedule consultation")
- Explain the offering: services overview, narrative pitch, or outcomes-led case-study lead — whichever matches the provided content
- Answer "why you": differentiators, methodology, values, or track record
- Humanize: a glimpse of the people or story, linking deeper
- Prove it (see Proof & Trust)
- End with a single clear, unaggressive contact path

**About page must:** tell the company story (history, founding, evolution), state mission/values, introduce key people with credentials, and surface awards/affiliations/location. Lead with whichever is strongest — founder story, team, or credentials.

**Services page must:** give each service a real description (approach, process, outcomes/deliverables), show pricing or "contact for quote", and invite conversation per service. Structure as methodology steps if a process was provided; include examples where case studies exist.

**Portfolio/Work page must:** show projects with name, brief description, and outcome; visuals where provided; filters if the body of work is diverse. Agencies lead with visuals; consultancies lead with metrics.

**Team page must:** photos, names, roles, credentials, brief backgrounds. Small team → rich bios with personality; large team → leadership featured; solo founder → fold into About.

**Contact page must:** a form (name, email, company, service interest, message), alternative methods (email/phone/address), a response-time commitment, and a map if location matters.

## Content Adaptation Rules

- **Full content** → extract positioning for the hero, distribute the rest per the jobs above, keep the consultative tone, suggest missing critical elements.
- **Partial content** → use as foundation; invoke **professional-copywriter** for gaps; ask for missing elements (services, team bios, history).
- **Minimal content** → invoke **professional-copywriter**; ask: services offered? team? years in business?

## Navigation

Primary: About · Services · Work/Portfolio · Team (if significant) · Contact. Footer: Blog/Resources, News, Careers, Privacy, Terms. Solo consultant trims to About/Services/Work/Contact; larger firms add Industries/Locations; creative agencies promote Work; B2B services add Resources for thought leadership.

## Messaging & Tone

- Trust-building and consultative: offer value, invite conversation — "Let's discuss your needs", never pushy
- Professional but approachable; warmth calibrated to industry
- **Consulting/professional services**: authoritative, strategic; credentials and results forward
- **Creative agency**: energetic, personality-driven; portfolio forward
- **B2B services/contractors**: straightforward, competent; reliability and certifications forward
- **Tech consulting**: technical but accessible

## CTA Strategy

1–2 CTAs per page — deliberately less aggressive than SaaS. Types: "Get in touch", "Schedule consultation", "Request proposal", "Let's talk about your project". Soft language, friction removed ("No obligation", "Free consultation"), zero fake urgency. Secondary: "View our work", "Read case studies", "Meet the team".

## Proof & Trust

In rough order of effectiveness: client testimonials with attribution → client logos → case studies (challenge/solution/results) → years in business → team credentials → industry affiliations → press/awards. **Never invent clients or numbers** — placeholder sections with a note to the user if none provided.

Trust signals: physical address (you're real), team bios with credentials, "Established [year]", association memberships, response-time commitment. Footer carries address/contact/badges; About carries history and credentials.

## SEO & Technical

- Title: `[Company] - [Services] in [Location]` for local businesses; meta covers services + audience + location
- Schema: Organization or LocalBusiness, Service, ContactPoint; location pages for multi-location firms
- Contact form: validation, spam protection, thank-you page; longer forms acceptable in this context
- Track relationship metrics (form submissions, phone/email clicks) over conversion funnels

## Additional Pages

Process/Methodology (step-by-step, what clients can expect) · Industries Served (sector-specific pages with per-industry proof) · FAQ (reduces contact friction) · Careers · News/Press. Every page keeps a subtle CTA. Avoid: aggressive sales tactics, jargon walls, hiding contact info, generic stock photos.
