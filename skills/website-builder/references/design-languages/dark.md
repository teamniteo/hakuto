# Dark Design Language

Principles reference — palettes and fonts are generated fresh per project (brand-designer color-wheel rules, fonts skill). Nothing here is a menu to copy.

## Aliases
Dark mode, Night mode, Black, Dark theme, Noir, Midnight

## Attitude

Sophisticated dark for any industry — premium, dramatic, elegant. Not a tech aesthetic and not inverted light mode: a deliberately composed dark scheme where hierarchy comes from brightness and depth, and the few bright elements carry all the weight.

## Principles

- Brightness is hierarchy: the most important element is the brightest; secondary content sits at 60–80% brightness
- Depth through layering — either progressively lighter surfaces or soft shadows, not both fighting
- Never pure white on pure black (halation); off-white text on near-black surfaces
- Accents used sparingly, so they glow by contrast without needing glow effects
- Motion smooth and elegant (300–600ms) — dark tolerates, and rewards, more choreography than light
- Body text slightly larger and looser than light-mode equivalents (readability tax of dark)

## Dial Presets

VARIANCE 5–7 · MOTION 4–7 · DENSITY 3–5
Push MOTION up for entertainment/creative brands; pull to 4 and VARIANCE to 5 for professional SaaS.

## Palette Strategy

Near-black base with a temperature — cool (blue-slate), warm (brown-black), or neutral — chosen to match the brand, varied per project. Triadic scheme; one bright accent for CTAs and key moments, everything else desaturated. Dark surfaces step lighter in 2–3 levels max. Status colors slightly desaturated so they don't scream against the dark field.

## Typography Strategy

Distinctive sans used decisively with hard weight contrast (400/800), or a literary serif display over a sans body for premium/luxury dark. Slightly heavier body weights than light mode (thin text disappears on dark).

## Watch Out

- Overused glow effects read amateur; one subtle glow moment max
- Inverting a light design is not a dark design — recompose contrast from scratch
- Don't confuse with Technology: Dark = sophisticated dark-mode for anyone; Technology = developer-specific with monospace/terminal heritage
- Test contrast rigorously — muddy mid-gray text on dark is the most common failure
