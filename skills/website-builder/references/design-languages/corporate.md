# Corporate Design Language

Principles reference — palettes and fonts are generated fresh per project (brand-designer color-wheel rules, fonts skill). Nothing here is a menu to copy.

## Aliases
Professional, Business, Enterprise, Formal, Traditional, Executive

## Attitude

Trust and credibility for decision-makers. Refined, not boring — polish and consistency signal competence the way a well-tailored suit does. Risk-averse in structure, confident in execution. Every design choice answers: "would a buyer's procurement team feel safe here?"

## Principles

- Credibility over creativity: predictable navigation, structured layouts, clear hierarchy
- Trust signals are content, not decoration — certifications, client logos, credentials, years in business, placed prominently
- Restrained motion (200–400ms, subtle) — nothing bouncy, nothing playful
- Photography professional and authentic; no handshake stock clichés
- Copy specific and data-backed; hype and exclamation marks erode the authority the design builds
- Detail discipline: consistent spacing, aligned grids, unified radius — sloppiness reads as risk

## Dial Presets

VARIANCE 3–5 · MOTION 2–3 · DENSITY 4–6
Traditional sectors (legal, finance) sit at VARIANCE 3; modern B2B tech can push to 5 with controlled asymmetry (7/5 splits, offset blocks) while staying professional.

## Palette Strategy

Conservative, low-saturation base — deep blues, charcoals, or warm neutral grays; vary which per project rather than defaulting to navy. Complementary scheme: one dependable primary plus a single warm or metallic contrast accent for CTAs. Light backgrounds dominate; saturated color stays under 10% of any viewport.

## Typography Strategy

Slab or literary serif display over a geometric sans body for established/traditional authority, or one modern geometric sans used decisively for tech-forward corporates. Weight contrast firm but not theatrical (400 body / 600–700 display). Generous line-height (1.7) — executives skim.

## Watch Out

- Corporate ≠ generic: the discipline is the aesthetic, but hue, type, and one asymmetric moment must still be chosen per project
- No experimental layouts — surprise is a cost, not a feature, for this audience
- Skipping trust signals defeats the entire language
- Casual/playful microcopy undermines everything the visuals establish
